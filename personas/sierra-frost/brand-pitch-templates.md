> disclosure handling. Pull this up before any pitch session.

**Current size baseline:** TikTok ~5K, IG ~3K, newsletter 0 (about to launch).

---

## 1. The disclosure conversation (read first)

Every brand pitch and deal must include AI disclosure to the brand BEFORE
they sign. This is the single most important rule in this doc.

### Why proactive disclosure protects the deal
- The 2024+ wave of FTC/state AI disclosure rules makes "we didn't know"
  legally untenable for brands. Brands with real legal teams are now
  *requiring* AI disclosure clauses in influencer contracts. You're not
  losing deals by disclosing — you're filtering for ones that won't blow up.
- "We told you upfront" is a complete defense if anything goes sideways.
- Many brands actively *prefer* AI personas (no person to manage, no
  off-brand behavior, perfect content control, no MeToo risk). Lean into
  this.

### The disclosure paragraph (paste into every first contact)

```
Quick note for transparency: Sierra Frost is a digital persona — AI-assisted
visuals with real opinions and real strategy behind every post. Her bio
discloses this and her audience knows. For your team this means: total
content control, zero off-brand behavior risk, and full FTC/state-AI-
disclosure compliance baked in. Happy to share more on how we structure
disclosed-AI partnerships.
```

If a brand says no after this — fine. They were going to be a problem
later. Move on.

### Pricing principles
- **Conservative niche pays 1.5–2x mainstream rates** for equivalent reach.
  Don't price like a generic 5K-follower lifestyle creator — price like a
  niche creator with a buying audience.
- **Bundles always win.** Single-post deals are throwaway. Push every brand
  to a bundle with at least 3 touchpoints across 7 days.
- **Newsletter is the highest-margin SKU** once the list is real. Bias
  pitches toward newsletter inclusion.

## 7. The negotiation playbook

### When they say "your rate is too high"

```
Totally hear you. Two ways we can work it:

1. Trim the bundle — we drop [X] and price comes to $[Y].
2. Add affiliate — we keep the bundle at $[reduced flat] + [%] commission
   on tracked sales for [60] days. Most of my partners come out ahead with
   option 2.

Which works better on your side?
```

### When they offer "exposure" / free product only

```
Appreciate the offer. I do 2 product-only collaborations per quarter for
brands I'm genuinely already using — would need to test [product] for 30
days before posting. If you'd rather skip the trial period, my floor for
paid is $[X].

Either works for me — your call.
```

### When they want exclusivity / category lock

```
Happy to grant exclusivity in [category] for [duration]. Standard exclusivity
premium is +30% on the base rate, given it locks me out of competitor deals.
Willing to discuss longer-term retainer if you want exclusivity baked in.
```

### When they ask "what guarantees do you give?"

```
I don't guarantee specific view or sale numbers — anyone who does is lying
to you. What I do guarantee:
- The post goes live within 24h of approved date
- It stays live for minimum 30 days
- Performance report 7 days post-launch
- 1 round of revisions on the concept before production

If a post under-performs, I'll happily discuss a make-good post on the next
campaign — this almost never comes up because conservative-niche
engagement is consistently above average.
```

### When they say "we don't work with AI creators"

```
Totally understand the hesitation. Two notes that often shift the calculus:

1. Disclosed-AI partnerships actually reduce your legal exposure vs. human
   influencers — there's no person to off-brand, no public misstep, no
   future scandal. The FTC requires disclosure either way; we make yours
   automatic.

2. Audience here knows. Disclosure has been in bio from day one and the
   audience self-selected in. Conversion is real because the takes are real.

Happy to share a sample contract that addresses your team's concerns.
Otherwise, all good — I appreciate the time.
```

### When they ghost after a verbal yes

After 7 days no response post-yes, send:

```
Hi [Name] — wanted to lock the [campaign] timeline. I'm holding [date]
open and need to confirm or release by [date+3]. If anything's shifted on
your end let me know and we'll find a new window.
```

## 8. The contract checklist

Before you sign anything, the contract must cover:

- [ ] **Deliverables specified exactly** (platforms, post count, format,
      length, story vs. feed, etc.)
- [ ] **Disclosure clause** — Sierra is AI-assisted, "must include #ad / paid
      partnership tag per FTC + platform rules" — explicit
- [ ] **Posting window** — defined, not "TBD"
- [ ] **Usage rights** — explicit. Default to organic-only on her channels.
      Anything more (whitelisting, brand-side ads) costs extra.
- [ ] **Approval rounds** — max 2 rounds of revisions, then post as-is
- [ ] **Payment terms** — 50% upfront, 50% within 14 days of post going
      live. Never "net 60." Never "pay on performance" unless it's a pure
      affiliate deal.
- [ ] **Kill fee** — 50% if brand cancels after content is created
- [ ] **No exclusivity unless paid for** — default is no category lock
- [ ] **Termination clause** — both sides can exit with 14 days notice
- [ ] **No "morality clause" that requires Sierra to stop posting opinions
      on her own pillars.** A clause that says "no controversial content
      during campaign window" is fine; one that limits her permanently is
      not.

If a brand pushes back on any of the above, that's a signal — proceed with
caution.

## 11. The "no" recovery

When a brand says no, immediately do this:

1. **Reply professionally within 1 day** — short and warm. "Totally
   understand, appreciate you considering. Mind if I check back in 3
   months? My audience grows ~30% per quarter."
2. **Add to the followup list** with date to recheck (90 days out).
3. **Don't take it personally** — most "no" decisions are budget timing,
   not creator fit. The 3-month recheck closes 20–30% of initial no's.

## 12. Cadence — what to do every week

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Monday | Send 5 cold DMs / emails to new brands |
| Tuesday | Follow up on 5 prior outreach with no response |
| Wednesday | Review affiliate dashboards, optimize linktr.ee |
| Thursday | Check newsletter sponsor inquiries, respond same day |
| Friday | Performance reports for any active campaigns |
| Sunday | Re-check the 3-month followup list, send recovery messages |

10 outreach actions per week = 40+/month. At a 5–10% response rate, that's
2–4 conversations a month. At a 30–50% conversion of conversations, that's
1–2 deals/month at current size.

## 13. What never to do

- ❌ Pitch a brand without disclosing the AI persona upfront
- ❌ Accept "exposure" as the only compensation
- ❌ Sign a "net 60" payment term
- ❌ Sign without a kill fee
- ❌ Accept a moral / behavior clause that limits Sierra's posting on her
  own pillars beyond the campaign window
- ❌ Promise specific view or sale numbers
- ❌ Pitch competitor brands the same week
- ❌ Lower a rate by more than 30% from initial ask — at that point you're
  just teaching them you were overpriced
- ❌ Cross-post the brand content as native (always disclose paid
  partnership)
- ❌ Take a deal that conflicts with another active deal in the same
  category
